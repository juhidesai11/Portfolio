Google Maps: Getting Everyone There Together

An independent product exploration. Not a claim about what Maps currently lacks, and not a claim that Google is building this.

Initial Assumption

Maps gets you there. It doesn’t get all of you there.

My favorite Google product is Google Maps. It solves a concrete problem at exactly the moment you need it. But when I’m meeting friends somewhere, Maps gets me there; coordinating everyone else still happens in a group text.

My starting assumption was that Maps was missing a way to see where your friends are relative to a shared destination, and that this was the gap worth designing for.

Research

Checking the assumption against the actual product

Before designing anything, I looked at what Maps already does. Current Maps already supports:

  • Location sharing
  • Trip progress sharing
  • ETA sharing
  • Location-sharing notifications
Discovery

My assumption was wrong

The visibility problem was already solved. What wasn’t there was any sense of the group as a group.

Each of those features is built around one person sharing one trip with one other person. There’s no shared destination, no shared target time, and no view of whether everyone is actually going to arrive together.

Reframed Problem

The unit of coordination is wrong

What if Maps treated the meetup, not each person’s individual trip, as the unit of coordination?

That reframing is the actual idea. Not “add location sharing” (it exists), but “give a group a shared object to coordinate around.”

Concept

Meetup Mode

Create a meetup → invite people → everyone joins → Maps coordinates the group’s arrival.

Potential product behaviors:

  • Show everyone’s ETA against the shared target time, in one place
  • Identify when someone may be running late
  • Suggest coordinated departure times so the group actually arrives together
  • Notify the group when plans need to adjust

Tradeoffs & Edge Cases

What gets harder, not just what gets better

  • Privacy: joining a meetup means sharing your live location with the whole group, not just one person. The consent model needs to be clearer, not just easier.
  • Partial participation: what happens when one person in the group won’t share location? The group view has to degrade gracefully, not break.
  • Notification fatigue: “Sarah is running late” is useful once; it’s noise if it fires every time an ETA shifts by a minute.
  • Battery impact: continuous location sharing across a whole group is more expensive than one-to-one sharing today.
  • When it ends: sharing needs to be scoped tightly to the meetup itself, with an obvious, automatic stop, not another setting people forget to turn off.
